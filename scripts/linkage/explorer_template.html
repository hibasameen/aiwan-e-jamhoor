Method

Sources. Candidate-level results: Colin Cookman's ECP compilations (GE 1993, 1997, 2002, 2008, 2013 and GE 2018) and the 2024 candidate-level scrape already used by the Aiwan-e-Jamhoor map (all 266 constituencies). Only general-election, direct (constituency) candidacies are included.

Record linkage. Cookman's candidateID is per-row, so persons were linked across elections by name matching: transliteration normalisation (Mohammad/Muhammad, Ch./Chaudhry, Bux/Bakhsh, Mahmood/Mehmood…), honorific stripping (Haji, Maulana, Sardar-as-title, ranks), then exact and fuzzy matching gated by geography (same district base), with looser thresholds only for long, distinctive names. Same-year multi-seat candidacies are merged when name and party match exactly. Curated closed groups protect major leaders (Imran Khan, the Sharifs, Benazir, Pervaiz Elahi…) from absorbing namesakes. Around 45 additional splits/merges were applied after manual web verification of the dynasty clusters.

Dynasty detection. Rare surname/clan tokens (person-level document frequency ≤ 120) in surname position, shared within a district, generate candidate family clusters; the top clusters plus curated cross-district families were then verified relationship-by-relationship against published sources. Only sourced relationships are drawn; same-clan namesakes are excluded.

Caveats

· Name-only linkage is imperfect. Very common short names (e.g. "Imran Khan", "Muhammad Aslam") can conflate different people in the same district, and a father and son with near-identical names can be merged. Persons spanning implausibly long careers should be treated with suspicion unless verified.

· Wins are GE wins in-window. By-elections, pre-1993 careers, reserved seats (women/minorities) and Senate careers are invisible here — e.g. Moonis Elahi's 2018 by-election win, Mamoona Hashmi's reserved seat, and everything before 1993 for veterans like Nawaz Sharif or Ghulam Ahmad Bilour.

· Covering candidacies. Feudal families routinely file token "covering" candidacies (a few hundred votes) in their own seats as insurance; these inflate contest counts and appear as family members contesting against each other.

· 2024 independents. Most 2024 independent winners were PTI-backed after the party lost its symbol; label-based switching and retention statistics for 2024 must be read in that light.

· Known source gaps. Cookman 1993/1997 files carry fewer candidates per seat (often top candidates only); 1997 NA-1 Peshawar is missing entirely; 1993 covers 203 of 207 general seats.

· Alliance vs party. MMA (2002, 2018), IJM (1993), National Alliance (2002) and GDA are electoral alliances; movement between an alliance and its constituent party is not defection.
