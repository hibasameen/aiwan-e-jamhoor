Analysis · National Assembly · 1977 – 2024

The Full Houseمکمل ایوان

Voters fill only the general seats. The rest of the House — today 70 seats, 60 for women and 10 for non-Muslims — has been filled four different ways since 1977: elected by the Assembly itself, by each province's MNAs, directly on separate electorates, and since 2002 from party lists in proportion to general seats won. This page draws every House as it was actually seated, works out what each party was entitled to under the list rule, sets that against what the Election Commission notified, and follows the women and non-Muslim members who have held those seats.

Entitlement versus allocation

The rule (Article 51(6)(d)–(e), section 104 of the Elections Act 2017): women's seats are shared out province by province — Punjab 35, Sindh 14, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa 8, Balochistan 3 until 2018; 32/14/10/4 from 2024 — in proportion to the general seats each party won in that province; the 10 non-Muslim seats nationwide in proportion to general seats won overall. Independents earn nothing unless they join a party within three days of the result, after which they count towards it. The seats of FATA and Islamabad carried no women's quota. The entitlement columns apply that rule to the parties as declared on election day, using the largest-remainder method; the ECP columns are the Commission's notified allocation. The difference is mostly the three-day window — the table says where.

Women in the House

Ten reserved seats in 1977, twenty in 1985 and 1988, none at all from 1990 to 1997 after the provision lapsed, sixty since 2002: the reserved quota has decided how many women sit far more than the ballot has. Women winning general seats stayed in single figures until 2002 — and in 1988 four women held nine of them, because Benazir Bhutto, Nusrat Bhutto and Abida Hussain each won more than one seat. The bars count seats held by women on election day; by-election winners are excluded, so a widely quoted figure of 16 for 2008 (which counts Faryal Talpur and Natasha Daultana, both returned at by-elections) appears here as 14.

Women elected on general seats

Outside Punjab and Sindh a woman has won a general seat only five times in eleven elections: Nasim Wali Khan's two Frontier seats in 1977, Zubaida Jalal twice in Balochistan's Kech, Shandana Gulzar once in Peshawar. Pre-2002 provinces are not recorded in the results for 1977–1990 and are left blank.

The non-Muslim seats

Six seats elected by the Assembly in 1977; ten from 1985 to 1997 elected directly by non-Muslim voters on separate rolls — four Christian, four Hindu and scheduled-caste, one Sikh/Buddhist/Parsi, one Ahmadi — with no party ticket; ten since 2002 allocated nationwide from party lists, so they track the largest parties. The same handful of names recur across the separate-electorate years. Only one non-Muslim candidate has won a general seat in this period: Mahesh Kumar Malani, for the PPP in Tharparkar, in 2018 and again in 2024.
