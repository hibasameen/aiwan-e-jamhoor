Analysis · National Assembly · 2002 – 2024

The Full Houseمکمل ایوان

Voters fill only the general seats. Another 70 — 60 for women, 10 for non-Muslims — are handed to parties in proportion to the general seats they won, from lists they filed before polling day. This page draws the House as it is actually seated, works out what each party was entitled to under the rule, sets that against what the Election Commission notified, and follows the women and non-Muslim members who hold those seats.

The House as seated

Every seat, general and reserved, coloured by party. Filled dots are general seats won at the poll; ringed dots are women's reserved seats; squares are non-Muslim reserved seats. Hover a seat for the member. The share columns show how the reserved seats change each party's weight in the House relative to its weight among elected seats.

Entitlement versus allocation

The rule (Article 51(6)(d)–(e), section 104 of the Elections Act 2017): women's seats are shared out province by province — Punjab 35, Sindh 14, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa 8, Balochistan 3 until 2018; 32/14/10/4 from 2024 — in proportion to the general seats each party won in that province; the 10 non-Muslim seats nationwide in proportion to general seats won overall. Independents earn nothing unless they join a party within three days of the result, after which they count towards it. The seats of FATA and Islamabad carried no women's quota. The entitlement columns apply that rule to the parties as declared on election day, using the largest-remainder method; the ECP columns are the Commission's notified allocation. The difference is mostly the three-day window — the table says where.

Women in the House

Since 2002 the 60 reserved seats have guaranteed women roughly a sixth of the House. The number who win general seats on their own account has stayed in single or low double digits. The bars count women seated on election day; by-election winners are excluded, so a widely quoted figure of 16 for 2008 (which counts Faryal Talpur and Natasha Daultana, both returned at by-elections) appears here as 14.

Women elected on general seats

By province the pattern is stark: outside Punjab and Sindh a woman has won a general seat only three times in five elections — Zubaida Jalal twice in Balochistan's Kech, Shandana Gulzar once in Peshawar.

The ten non-Muslim seats

Allocated nationwide, so they track the largest parties. Between 1985 and 1997 non-Muslim members were instead elected directly on separate electorates — those results are not on this page (see Method). Only one non-Muslim candidate has won a general seat in this period: Mahesh Kumar Malani, for the PPP in Tharparkar, in 2018 and again in 2024.
